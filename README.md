## Overview

### Structure

The format is quite straightforward: most important information usually is in the begining of the section, the rest are usually derivation.
There are also some code on `Rss/QM/code` about my research on quantum mechanics.


### Content

Beside quantum mechanics, this text also contain nuclear physics, modern physics, classical mechanics, electrodynamics, and statistical mechanics.


**Quantum Mechanics** chapter discuss:

1. **Quantum Mechanics Postulate:** Introduces the fundamental postulates of quantum mechanics, including quantum states, observables, measurement, and time evolution.
2. **Application to Simple System:** Discusses standard quantum-mechanical systems, including free particles, potential wells and barriers, harmonic oscillators, and the hydrogen atom.
3. **Spin:** Covers angular momentum, spin, the Dirac and Pauli equations, and Heisenberg spin-chain models.
4. **Classical Information:** Introduces Shannon entropy, data compression, the law of large numbers, and several measures of classical information.
5. **Quantum Information:** Discusses density operators, the Bloch sphere, quantum bits, quantum gates, entanglement, fidelity, and ergotropy.
6. **Second Quantization:** Covers the quantization of electromagnetic fields, Fock and coherent states, creation and annihilation operators, and quantum field operators.
7. **Many-Body System:** Introduces many-particle operators, identical particles, the variational principle, Hartree and Hartree–Fock methods, and graphene.
8. **Solid State:** Discusses crystal lattices, crystal structures, bonding, and basic models of electrons in solids.
9. **Research Note: State Transfer:** Notes on my current research. Basically application of quantum state transfer in spin chains.

**Nuclear Physics** chapter discusses:

1. **Introduction:** Introduces atomic species, nucleons, and the relationships between isotopes, isobars, and isotones.
2. **Radioactivity:** Covers nuclear decay, alpha and beta decay, radioactive activity, half-life, and nuclear stability.
3. **Particle Physics:** Introduces elementary particles, including bosons, fermions, and hadrons, along with their quantum numbers and conservation laws.
4. **Nuclear Model:** Discusses the liquid drop model and its contributions to nuclear binding energy and the stability of atomic nuclei.

**Modern Physics** chapter discusses:

1. **Modern Physics Chronology:** Summarizes the historical development of modern physics and the discoveries that changed our understanding of nature.
2. **Relativity:** Covers Einstein's postulates, Lorentz transformations, spacetime geometry, proper time, and relativistic kinematics.
3. **Quantum Mechanics:** Introduces the foundations of quantum mechanics, from the photoelectric effect and atomic models to wavefunctions, the Schrödinger equation, and simple quantum systems.
4. **Atom:** Discusses atomic structure, quantum numbers, electron configurations, the periodic table, atomic wavefunctions, and the Zeeman effect.
5. **Statistical Mechanics:** Introduces the Boltzmann distribution, equipartition theorem, quantum statistics, blackbody radiation, and the Maxwell speed distribution.
6. **Wave:** Covers wave functions, standing and traveling waves, harmonic oscillations, interference, diffraction, Bragg's law, and the electromagnetic spectrum.

**Classical Mechanics** chapter discusses:

1. **Unsorted Classical Mechanics Topics:** Collects basic mechanics concepts, including Newton's laws, rotational motion, energy, momentum, collisions, and gravitation.
2. **Central Force:** Covers the equations of motion under central forces, orbital trajectories, and Kepler's laws.
3. **Energy:** Discusses potential energy and its application to gravitational systems.
4. **Lagrangian Mechanics:** Introduces the Lagrangian formulation, constrained motion, Lagrange multipliers, and applications to mechanical systems.
5. **Hamiltonian Mechanics:** Covers Hamilton's equations, canonical transformations, Poisson brackets, and Hamiltonian formulations of mechanical systems.
6. **Simple Harmonic Motion:** Discusses mass-spring systems, pendulums, and the connection between oscillatory motion and other physical systems.
7. **Damped Harmonic Oscillation:** Covers damped motion, energy dissipation, RLC circuits, and the quality factor.
8. **Forced Oscillation:** Discusses driven oscillations, resonance, mechanical impedance, and power absorption.
9. **Traveling Waves:** Introduces the wave equation, traveling waves, vibrating strings, wave energy, impedance, and wave propagation in different dimensions.
10. **Standing Waves:** Covers standing waves, their formation through superposition, energy, normal modes, and Fourier analysis.
11. **Interference:** Discusses Huygens' principle, double-slit interference, interferometers, diffraction, beats, dispersion, and wave packets.

**Electrodynamics** chapter discusses:

1. **Electrostatics:** Introduces electric fields, electric potential, electrostatic energy, conductors, and capacitors.
2. **Potential:** Covers Laplace's equation, uniqueness theorems, the method of images, separation of variables, and multipole expansion.
3. **Electric Field in Matter:** Discusses dielectrics, polarization, bound charges, electric displacement, and energy in dielectric systems.
4. **Magnetostatics:** Covers magnetic forces, current distributions, the Biot–Savart law, Ampère's law, magnetic vector potential, and magnetic dipoles.
5. **Magnetic Field in Matter:** Introduces magnetization, paramagnetism, diamagnetism, bound currents, and ferromagnetism.
6. **Electrodynamics:** Discusses Ohm's law, electromotive force, electromagnetic induction, inductance, Maxwell's equations, and electric circuits.
7. **Conservation Laws:** Derives the conservation laws for electric charge, energy, and momentum in electromagnetic systems.
8. **Electromagnetic Waves:** Covers the electromagnetic wave equation, plane waves, wave propagation in matter, and reflection and transmission.
9. **Introduction to Optics:** Introduces Fermat's principle, the law of reflection, and Snell's law.
10. **Optical Geometry:** Discusses image formation using spherical surfaces, thin lenses, mirrors, and prisms.

**Statistical Mechanics** chapter discusses:

1. **Key Concepts:** Introduces the basic concepts of thermodynamics, including ideal gases, Carnot engines, specific heat, internal energy, and entropy.
2. **Thermodynamics' Laws:** Covers the zeroth, first, second, and third laws of thermodynamics and their implications for physical systems.
3. **Thermodynamics Postulate:** Introduces the fundamental postulates of thermodynamics and derives properties such as temperature, pressure, and chemical potential.
4. **Thermodynamics' Potentials:** Discusses the Helmholtz potential, Gibbs potential, enthalpy, and grand potential, including their physical meanings and thermodynamic relations.
5. **Maxwell Relations:** Covers Maxwell relations and the fundamental and primary sets of thermodynamic relations.
6. **Introduction to Statistical Mechanics:** Introduces quantum statistics, discrete systems, microstates, and the statistical description of physical systems.
7. **Maxwell-Boltzmann Distribution:** Explores the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution through several derivations and discusses its application to particle velocities and other physical quantities.
8. **Bose-Einstein Distribution:** Covers the statistical distribution of bosons, phonons, and the Einstein and Debye models of solids.
9. **Planck Distribution:** Discusses blackbody radiation, Planck's distribution, and related laws, including the Rayleigh-Jeans, Wien, and Stefan-Boltzmann laws.
10. **Fermi-Dirac Distribution:** Introduces fermionic statistics and examines the properties of a Fermi gas at zero and low temperatures.
11. **Boltzmann Entropy:** Explores Boltzmann's statistical definition of entropy and its applications to discrete energy levels, two-level systems, continuous energy distributions, and phase space.
12. **Equilibrium Distribution:** Covers ensembles, the partition function, probabilities, thermodynamic quantities, the Gibbs paradox, and rotational and vibrational partition functions.
